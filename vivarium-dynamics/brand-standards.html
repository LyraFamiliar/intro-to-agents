§1.0 Brand Truth

Vivarium Dynamics sells living creatures that hatch from an egg — each one genetically unlike any that has existed before. We are not a novelty company. We are a laboratory that believes it has done something wonderful, and says so without irony.

We declared war on chance, and we lose every single time — in public, one Specimen at a time.

Every claim we make is sincere. Every claim is also, in some small measure, untrue, because a Specimen always becomes something we did not specify. This is the engine of the brand: an institution that loves these animals so completely it believes it can author them, and keeps selling you the next attempt.

Never wink. The moment our copy acknowledges the gap between what we promise and what arrives, the brand collapses into parody. We do not joke. We are proud, thorough, and slightly wrong.

§2.0 The Adversary

Our competitor is not another laboratory. It is randomness.

Before Vivarium, a living thing was whatever accident made it. We industrialised the lottery: coloration, character, and habit selected deliberately, so that nothing about your Specimen is left to luck. That is the promise, and it is the reason the work matters.

It follows that rarity is not a product. We do not sell odds, we do not advertise pull rates, and we do not price one egg above another. Every egg costs $4.99. When a Specimen arrives unusual, that is not a prize we awarded — it is a result we did not intend, recorded honestly in the Registry.

The Registry is a log of outcomes, not a rarity market. Write it as documentation. Never as a leaderboard.

§3.0 Voice

Institutional sincerity. The register of a research organisation writing to someone it trusts with a living thing: warm, precise, faintly formal, never cute. Specimens are observed and recorded, never “played with.” Dormancy is a phase.

Correct

  • “Your Specimen is unlike any Specimen in recorded history.”
  • “Dormancy is a natural phase of the Specimen lifecycle.”
  • “Raise it well. Record what it becomes.”
  • “Variance is reported voluntarily.”

Prohibited

  • Any acknowledgement that the Specimen is small, simple, or disappointing.
  • Humour at the Specimen’s expense, or at our own.
  • The words die, dead, death, pet, toy, gimmick.
  • Exclamation marks in Registry correspondence.

Nomenclature

Specimen is always capitalised and always singular when addressing an owner. Registry and Legacy Services carry the trademark on first use per surface. The company is Vivarium Dynamics — never abbreviated to VD in customer-facing copy, for reasons the Office considers self-evident.

§9.0 Anomalous Specimens

Restricted — Deviation Orange permitted

A Specimen exhibiting deviation from intended expression was failed at the moment of authorship. It did not choose its condition. Neither did we.

The Voluntary Specimen Recovery Program™ offers complimentary Preservation, expedited collection, and a $200 Registry credit for any such Specimen. This is a rescue. It is never to be described as a recall, a buy‑back, or a defect claim.

“An Anomalous Specimen did not choose its condition. Neither did we. Let us take it home.”

M. Kessler · Owner’s Manual, 4th edition

Recovery is offered in good faith. Owners who decline are not to be contacted a second time. Correspondence regarding unreported deviation is handled by the Office directly and does not appear in Registry records.

Materials produced by Fen Biologic are not to be referenced, rebutted, or acknowledged in any Vivarium surface. Where the founding team is described, the approved phrasing is “a former member of the founding team.” No name is to be printed.

§10.0 Prohibitions

  • P‑01No wild release. Under no circumstances is a Specimen to be introduced to an uncontained environment. Containment is not a safety measure; it is the whole of our purpose. Outside, chance resumes.
  • P‑02No sale of rarity. No premium egg, no graded pack, no published odds, no purchasable advantage.
  • P‑03No duplicate authorship. A retired genome signature is never reissued. Uniqueness is not a feature; it is a commitment.
  • P‑04No irony. Not in copy, not in art direction, not in the fine print. We mean all of it.
